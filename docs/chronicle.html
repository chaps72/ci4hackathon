White House science funding overhaul
The White House unveiled a science funding overhaul in late July 2026 that called for reducing university research funding, steering money outside academia, boosting AI, and reforming a system it called 'calcified.' The plan quickly drew bipartisan pushback, with Senate Republicans and other senators opposing political-appointee control over funding and appropriators advancing a stopgap bill to block the changes. It now faces significant Senate resistance and ongoing concern from scientists, leaving the proposed changes contested and uncertain.

Pentagon blacklist of foreign universities
Beginning in late July 2026, the Pentagon expanded its blacklist to include China's top civilian universities, followed by US sanctions on leading Chinese universities and research institutes over national security concerns, prompting warnings that the list could threaten the US science edge. The scrutiny has since turned inward: on August 17, the Department of War ordered research security audits at 30 US academic institutions to examine their foreign research ties. The saga now sits at a domestic enforcement phase, meaning US universities face heightened compliance pressure and potential disruption to international research collaborations.

On July 24, 2026, HHS rescinded portions of its Title VI civil rights regulations to align with statutory text and Executive Order 14281, followed on August 4 by a related NSF nondiscrimination rule in federally assisted programs. These changes signal an evolving federal civil rights compliance landscape for grant-receiving institutions that the office should review against current policies.

Keyword-based research grant terminations
The White House has admitted to using keyword-based lists to terminate billions of dollars' worth of California research grants, with subsequent reporting exposing the contents of these federal keyword lists. The revelations show that grant cancellations were driven by automated or list-based screening rather than case-by-case review, raising significant concerns for institutions whose funding may have been flagged by targeted terms.
