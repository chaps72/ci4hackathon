AHRQ grant terminations
Beginning July 20, 2026, reports emerged that AHRQ had terminated dozens of patient care research grants, with cancellations initially reported at $109 million and subsequently escalating to over $131 million across 28 states. The freeze has reportedly halted patient safety research nationwide and raised broader fears about the future of U.S. health services research. For the research office, this represents a significant and growing threat to funding for healthcare quality and patient safety work spanning multiple states.

Harvard research funding freeze and legal challenge
As of July 27, 2026, three dozen of Harvard's peer institutions have filed to back its court fight against the Trump administration over its research funding freeze. The case remains in litigation, and the broad support signals that other universities view the outcome as consequential for the wider research community.

Keyword-based research grant terminations
The White House has admitted to using keyword-based lists to terminate billions of dollars' worth of California research grants, with subsequent reporting exposing the contents of these federal keyword lists. The revelations show that grant cancellations were driven by automated or list-based screening rather than case-by-case review, raising significant concerns for institutions whose funding may have been flagged by targeted terms.

HHS Title VI civil rights regulation rollback
On July 24, 2026, HHS rescinded portions of its Title VI civil rights regulations, stating the goal was to align them with the statutory text and conform to Executive Order 14281. This narrows the scope of HHS civil rights regulations, which may affect compliance obligations tied to federally funded research.

White House science funding overhaul
The White House unveiled a major science funding overhaul accompanied by a report characterizing the U.S. science system as 'calcified' and calling for reduced university research funding, with a stated push to steer research support outside academia and toward new AI funding initiatives. The plan signals a potential structural shift in how federal research dollars are allocated, with universities positioned to lose funding share under the proposed changes.
