AHRQ Grant Cancellations Escalate Fears Over Future of U.S. Health Services Research
Healthcare Innovation (HCI Innovation Group) · 2026-07-29 · press coverage
AcademyHealth reports AHRQ has not issued a new research grant since April 2025, roughly 80 percent of its workforce has departed, and nearly every active grant has been terminated. The group estimates more than 80 awards worth over $200 million have been canceled, affecting patient-safety, quality-improvement, and workforce studies.
Emory impact · LOW severity
Emory's health services research is concentrated in Rollins School of Public Health and clinical/quality-improvement work in the School of Medicine, both of which can hold AHRQ awards for patient-safety and quality-improvement studies. If AHRQ has terminated nearly all active grants, any Emory AHRQ-funded projects face abrupt stop-work, unrecovered costs, and disrupted research staff. AHRQ is a small share of Emory's overall federal portfolio relative to NIH, so enterprise-wide exposure is limited.
SVPR office: OSVPR should inventory any active or pending AHRQ awards across Rollins and SOM to quantify at-risk funding and prepare bridge-funding or wind-down guidance for affected PIs.
Odds of winning NIH grants plummet as new funding policy and spending delays bite
Science (AAAS) · 2026-07-29 · press coverage
Science reports that NIH grant success rates are falling sharply—at the National Cancer Institute from roughly 1 in 10 to 1 in 25—driven by spending delays and a new policy requiring multiyear grants to be funded with a single upfront lump sum. Fourteen GOP senators sent a July 25 letter to OMB Director Russ Vought demanding NIH be allowed to spend its full appropriated 2025 budget.
Emory impact · HIGH severity
NIH is the dominant source of Emory's federal research funding, so falling success rates and lump-sum upfront funding requirements hit the entire enterprise—especially Winship Cancer Institute, where NCI paylines reportedly worsened dramatically. Lower award rates threaten renewals and new investigator funding across SOM, Rollins, and the Vaccine Center, while lump-sum accounting changes complicate multiyear budgeting and cash-flow planning.
SVPR office: OSVPR must own the portfolio-level financial and cash-flow risk of the lump-sum policy and coordinate an institutional response, including bridge funding and guidance for PIs facing worsened paylines.
US sanctions top Chinese universities and research institutes, citing national security threat
Breakthrough News · 2026-07-29 · press coverage
The U.S. Defense Department added leading Chinese universities and research organizations to a blacklist of foreign institutions deemed potential national-security threats over alleged military links. The July 23 list includes 130 institutions—88 of them Chinese, including Fudan and Shanghai Jiao Tong—along with entities in Russia and Iran.

The DOD blacklist of Chinese, Russian, and Iranian institutions affects Emory research collaborations, subawards, and foreign-national personnel tied to listed entities, particularly in areas with DOD funding and any international co-investigators. Emory's research security and foreign-influence compliance obligations increase, requiring review of existing collaborations and disclosures against the updated list.
SVPR office: OSVPR should direct a compliance review to screen current collaborations, subrecipients, and personnel against the July 23 list and update research-security screening procedures.
$47 million program offers hybrid university-industry Ph.D. training
STAT News · 2026-07-29 · press coverage
The Trump administration announced a pilot program in which NSF will spend $47 million over five years to support roughly 250 students in four-year PhD programs jointly funded by universities and industry partners. The initiative reflects the White House's broader push to steer federal research funding toward industry-linked models.
Emory impact · LOW severity
The NSF hybrid university-industry PhD pilot is modest in scale and NSF is a secondary funder for Emory, so direct financial impact is limited. However, Emory's STEM and biomedical PhD programs could compete for these industry-linked training slots, and the initiative signals a broader shift toward industry-partnered funding models that may affect future training-grant strategy.
SVPR office: OSVPR should note the industry-linked funding trend for strategic planning but need not mount a specific institutional response yet.
